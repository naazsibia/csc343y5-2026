What was this course about?

CSC343 is an introduction to database management systems. Over the term we explored how data is modelled, stored, queried, and kept consistent — and why those problems are more challenging than they look.

🗂Data modelling — how to represent real-world entities and their relationships using ERDs and the relational model
🔍Querying — relational algebra as the mathematical foundation, and SQL as the practical language you'll use in the real world
🏗Database design — functional dependencies, normal forms, and how to avoid the anomalies that come from poor schema design
⚙️System internals — indexes, query optimisation, and how the database engine actually executes your queries
🔐Transactions & concurrency — ACID properties, isolation levels, locking, and what happens when two users touch the same data at once

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you were able to:

  • Design entity-relationship diagrams (ERDs) that accurately model complex, real-world domains
  • Translate ERDs to relational schemas and implement them in PostgreSQL
  • Write correct and efficient SQL queries, including aggregation, subqueries, joins, and NULL handling
  • Express queries in relational algebra (RA) and understand the connection between RA and SQL
  • Identify functional dependencies and normalise a schema to BCNF or 3NF
  • Explain how indexes and query optimisation work, and make informed design decisions
  • Reason about transactions, isolation levels, and concurrency using 2PL and MVCC
  • Situate relational databases within the broader landscape of modern data systems (NoSQL, vector DBs, distributed systems)

Course Philosophy: Students First

I believe the best learning happens when students feel supported, not stressed. A few principles that shaped how this course was run:

Better later is still better

The 5% between the midterm and final always shifted to whichever score was higher. If you struggled on the midterm but put in the work and did better on the final, that growth is reflected in your grade.

Good work takes iteration

Every Checkpoint had a resubmission window. Students who revised their work generally learned more than students who moved on right away.

Each piece builds the next

Every assignment used the same Research Network. The context built over time so that everything connected.

Low-stakes entry

Checkpoint 1 was intentionally small and forgiving. The goal was to orient you.

Partial credit beats zero

If you were at risk of missing a deadline, submitting whatever you had was always worth more than a zero.

Please reach out

If something went wrong — personally, academically, or otherwise — I'm still glad you talked to me. That door stays open even now that the term is done.

Format requirement: All written solutions were required to be typeset in LaTeX. Missing files, non-compiling submissions, or syntax-error-only work received a grade of 0.

Policies

Academic Integrity
All Checkpoints were individual work. Sharing or viewing another student's code was not permitted. Discussions were required to stay conceptual — no solutions, no walkthroughs of approach.
Plagiarism detection (MOSS, JPLAG, TurnItIn) ran on every submission.
AI tools (QuickTA, VoiceEx) were permitted as learning aids only. All submitted work had to be entirely the student's own. Submitting AI-generated content without citation counted as plagiarism. Hallucinated sources counted as fabrication. Both are academic offences.
Tests and the final exam did not permit AI.
Academic integrity investigations persist even after the course ends or is dropped. If found guilty, a student may be re-enrolled and receive a failing grade.
Missed Work & Accommodations
Term workEmail the Course Coordinator with supporting documentation at least 1 week in advance, or within 1 week of an unexpected event. Declared illness on ACORN. Not automatic.
Missed midtermNo accommodations. In a serious documented emergency, the Course Coordinator was to be contacted immediately.
Missed final examOnline petition required within 72 hours. Late petitions were not considered.
Religious holidaysInstructor notified at least 2 weeks before the relevant due date.
AccessibilityAccommodations were arranged through Accessibility Services before writing any assessment. Retroactive petitions were not accepted.
Resubmissions & Regrades
Each Checkpoint allowed one resubmission (Checkpoint 1: within 48 hrs; Checkpoints 2–5: within 1 week of grade release). Resubmitted work was graded out of a maximum of 80%; the recorded grade was the higher of the two attempts.
Teaching staff could conduct oral interviews after any due date to verify understanding. Interview grades were final and could not be appealed.
Re-mark requests are still open: submit on MarkUs within 5 calendar days of receiving a grade. Re-marking may cover the full submission and may raise or lower the mark. This is the one policy that still actively applies post-term — don't sit on it.
PCRS Homework
Was required to be completed before the registered lecture section. No late submissions were accepted and no accommodations were made.
Full marks required: all exercises correct, all videos watched (or readings done), all reflections and follow-up components, any external surveys, and any linked platform activities.
